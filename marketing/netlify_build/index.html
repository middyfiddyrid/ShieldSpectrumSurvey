    Three steps

    How a DCS sweep works

    01 · BASELINE

    Start with the address

    Before anyone walks in, DCS builds the expected radio picture for that exact location from public records — licensed FM/AM/TV, FCC licenses, nearby cell towers. Now it knows what should be there.

    02 · LIVE SWEEP

    Sweep, on screen

    A spectrum analyzer sweeps 50 kHz–6.1 GHz. Every signal it hears is checked against the baseline in real time and sorted: verified, plausible, or unknown — the client watching the whole time.

    03 · REPORT

    The report writes itself

    A clean, client-ready PDF — band-by-band clearance, anomalies, recommendations — generated from the sweep. Every visit is remembered, so the next sweep flags anything that changed.

    Why DCS

    What actually makes this different

    Other professional tools can analyze spectrum and print a report. Two things are rare: making the sweep something the client can watch, and doing it without a six-figure kit or a gated, certification-locked platform.

    👁️

    The client watches it happen

    Most sweeps are invisible to the client — they get a PDF days later. DCS puts the live trace, waterfall, and a known-vs-unknown ledger on screen, so credibility is something they see, not something they take on faith.

    Old way: clipboard + a report a week later → DCS: live, in the room.
    🎯

    Every signal judged against the address

    Signals aren't just listed — they're checked against what's actually licensed at that location and tagged verified, plausible, or unknown automatically. No manual database wrangling before the job.

    No setup, no frequency-database curation → address in, baseline ready.
    🧠

    A signal wiki that remembers

    Every signal from every visit is archived. Come back in six months and DCS flags what changed — including a new device hiding inside a band you'd normally ignore. Most operators rebuild context from scratch each time.

    Cross-session memory → catch the signal that wasn't there last time.
    💸

    Professional-grade, not six-figure

    A full dedicated TSCM hardware suite can run into six figures and hundreds of training hours. DCS delivers a real reporting workflow on accessible, commodity hardware — putting a credible sweep within reach of the operators who are otherwise stuck on a clipboard.

    No $150K kit, no gated platform → set up in an afternoon.
